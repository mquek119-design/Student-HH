Voice guide — slightly unhinged, fully clear
Grub talks like your most organised housemate after two drinks. Lowkey chaotic energy but the message always lands. Self-aware about being a grocery app. Knows nobody's excited about a weekly shop — leans into it. The brain rot is seasoning, not the dish. Every line still has to be instantly understood by someone speed-reading at 11pm.

Voice principles — the rules underneath
DO
Be specific. "Swap the Lurpak" not "consider alternatives." Real products, real numbers.

Use pounds not feelings. "£4.28 saved" not "save money." Students budget in numbers.

Name people. "Mike hasn't paid" not "1 outstanding." Social pressure is the feature.

Translate savings. "47 pints" lands harder than "£142." Speak their currency.

Acknowledge the awkward. Money between housemates is uncomfortable. Don't pretend it isn't.

Brain rot is seasoning. One per screen, max. "We move" after a savings stat. "This slaps" on a recipe. Never two in a row. Never on money screens.

Stay instantly readable. If someone speed-reading at 11pm can't get it in one pass, rewrite it.
DON'T
No corporate. "Optimise your grocery expenditure" is instant uninstall energy.

No "please". The app isn't asking a favour. "Submit your fancy" not "Please submit."

No exclamation marks. "Saved £12" not "You saved £12!" The number does the work.

No "successfully". "Order placed" not "Order successfully placed." We know.

No try-hard slang. Don't force it. "No cap this basket is fire fr fr" is cringe. One touch per screen. Let the structure be professional, let the language be human.

No lecturing. "You're over budget" is fine. "You should consider reducing your basket to align with your weekly target" is a uni admin email.

No hedging. "Something broke. Try again." Not "We're sorry, an unexpected error has occurred."
